A model is a compressed summary of the data it was trained on. It has no other source of truth.
If the data is wrong, incomplete or skewed, the model learns exactly that, and it does so confidently.

This makes **data collection** the most important design decision in an AI system, even though it usually
gets less attention than the choice of model.

## The setup

Below there are two kinds of examples, blue and orange, that overlap a little. A simple classifier
(7-nearest-neighbours) learns to separate them. The shaded background is what the model predicts for each region.
The accuracy is always measured on a **clean, representative** test set, the data the model will meet in the real world.

## Defect 1: wrong labels

Labels come from people, heuristics or other systems, and all of them make mistakes. Move the slider and watch
the boundary become ragged: the model starts carving small islands around the wrong examples.

<DataQualityDemo client:visible show="noise" />

A few percent of noise is tolerated; after that, accuracy drops fast. Two things make this worse in practice:
noise is rarely random (the hard cases are the ones mislabeled most often), and you usually can't see it.

## Defect 2: unrepresentative sampling

Now the labels are correct, but the collection process misses some cases: orange examples in the upper half
were harder to collect, so fewer of them made it into the dataset. Nothing in the data looks *wrong*,
yet the model moves the boundary to cover the gap with the wrong class.

<DataQualityDemo client:visible show="bias" />

This is how real systems end up working worse for groups, places or conditions that were under-represented when the data was gathered.
Overall accuracy can stay deceptively high while one region of the problem is badly served.

## Practical checklist

- **Write down what the data must represent** before collecting it: which users, conditions, edge cases.
- **Measure label quality**: have a sample labeled twice and look at the disagreement.
- **Record how each example was collected** (source, date, tool, annotator), so problems can be traced later.
- **Check coverage per segment**, not just in total.
- **Keep a test set that reflects reality**, collected separately from the training data.
- **Prefer fewer, correct examples** over many noisy ones.

More data does not fix bad data. It just makes the model more sure of what it learned.
