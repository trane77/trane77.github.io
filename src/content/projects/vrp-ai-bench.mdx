Planning routes for a small fleet of delivery vans is a classic hard problem: every stop must be visited once,
within its time window, without exceeding capacity or driver hours, while keeping time and fuel low.

VRP AI Bench is a **research laboratory** built to answer one question honestly:
*does a learned search policy find better routes, or find them faster, than classical heuristics at the same time budget?*

## Watch a search improve a plan

The engine starts from a quick feasible plan and improves it move by move, and every change is recorded so the search can be replayed.
Here is a much simpler version you can run in the browser. Press **Run search**.

<RouteSearchDemo client:visible />

In the real engine the moves are relocation, swap and reversal across routes, every candidate is validated against time windows and capacity,
and the objective weighs travel time against estimated fuel. The learned policy decides **which move to try next**: it doesn't
replace the solver, it reorders its choices.

## What's in the lab

- **Real cities, real roads**: Bologna and Modena on a frozen OpenStreetMap snapshot served by OSRM, including alternative road paths.
- **Realistic constraints**: 1–5 vans with different capacities, appointment time windows, mandatory driver breaks, mixed pickups and deliveries.
- **Fair comparisons**: the same move operators, evaluator and budgets for classical and learned search, plus OR-Tools as an independent reference. Train/validation/test sets and seeds are frozen before evaluation.
- **Learned policy**: a small neural network (38 features → 64 → 32 → 2) that ranks candidate moves, trained on recorded searches.
- **Robustness**: Monte Carlo evaluation of stochastic travel times, and traffic/emissions microsimulation (SUMO) to calibrate the fuel model.
- **Observable search**: a React client with an interactive map, per-van timelines, comparison charts and replay of every recorded move.
- **Remote compute**: the engine runs as an authenticated HTTPS/WebSocket service on a separate machine on the local network.

## Being honest about results

The protocol is strict on purpose: no optimality claims from heuristics, time-outs reported as *unknown* rather than dropped,
training cost counted separately, and negative results treated as valid findings.
So far the infrastructure and a 336-case held-out campaign are complete, and **no measured AI advantage is claimed**.
